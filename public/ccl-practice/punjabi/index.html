About the NAATI CCL Punjabi test

The Credentialed Community Language (CCL) test assesses your ability to interpret spoken dialogue between English and Punjabi — one of Australia’s fastest-growing migrant languages. It consists of two dialogues of around 300 words each, each split into segments of up to 35 words that you interpret one at a time. To pass you need at least 29 marks out of 45 across both dialogues.

Passing the Punjabi CCL test earns 5 bonus points toward the points-tested skilled migration visas (subclasses 189, 190 and 491) — often the difference that gets an application over the line.

Our platform lets you practise Punjabi CCL dialogues across the exact domains you will face on exam day: health and medical, legal, employment, housing, education, banking and finance, immigration, and community and social services.

How is the NAATI CCL Punjabi test scored?+

The CCL test uses two dialogues of about 300 words each, split into segments you interpret between English and Punjabi. Each segment is marked on a 0–5 scale. You need 29 out of 45 (about 63%) across both dialogues to pass. Our AI scores every segment against the official criteria so you always know where you stand.

How many points is the NAATI CCL Punjabi test worth for Australian PR?+

Passing the CCL test earns 5 bonus points toward the points-tested skilled migration visas — subclasses 189, 190 and 491 — under Australia's SkillSelect system.
